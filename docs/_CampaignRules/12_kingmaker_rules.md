# Special Rules for Kingmaker

> The rules on this page are temporary rules for the Kingmaker campaign. Anything here is not officially part of the Varlyn ruleset, but is intended to be compatible with it. Perhaps we like the rules well enough to incorporate them into the Varlyn ruleset in the future.

# Hexploration

During the Kingmaker campaign, the party explores the Stolen Lands by traveling from hex to hex on the regional map. Each hex represents a 12‑mile hexagon and the party can normally move only to adjacent hexes. The rules on this page combine the official Kingmaker hexploration rules with the site’s travel and adventuring rules, streamlined for play.

Each day of hexploration is divided into a set number of activities. The party uses these activities to Travel or Reconnoiter. When the party camps, additional rules cover preparing a campsite, cooking special meals, keeping watch and dealing with weather.

Each day begins with **[Breaking Camp](../RulesExtra/travel.html#internal-BreakingCamp)**, which takes 30 minutes. During this time, the party checks the weather, assigns travel roles for the day, sets their pace and packs up their gear. If the party wishes to remain in the same location, they must Prepare the Campsite again; a previously used site takes only 1 hour to prepare and may not require a new check, depending on circumstances.

## Daily Structure

Each day of hexploration consists of:

- Paying **HA** for **Travel** through the hex (based on terrain), plus optionally spending additional HA on **Reconnoiter**.
- On arrival at camp, each character performs one activity from each of the following categories:
  - **1 Pre-camp Activity**
  - **1 Large Rest Activity**
  - **1 Small Rest Activity**



## Amount of Hexploration Activities

The number of hexploration activities (HAs) available is determined by the party's travel pace. The default is **2 activities per day**, but this can be increased or decreased by the party's pace, weather and other factors. No less than 1 HA and no more than 4 HAs are available per day. The party may choose to spend all of their HAs on Travel or they may split them between Travel and Reconnoitering.

<div class="columnsthree">

- #### Pace
  The party may choose to perform activities at a careful, normal or reckless pace. The pace affects the number of hexploration activities received, the ability to hide tracks and the ability to use stealth. See the table below for details.

- <div class="block classTable frame" markdown="1">

  #### Traveling
  | Pace | HA | Guide | Forage | Lookout |
  |:-----|:--:|:-----:|:------:|:-------:|
  | Careful | 1 | Adv. | Normal | Adv. |
  | Normal | 2 | Normal | Disadv. | Normal |
  | Reckless | 3 | Disadv. | None | Disadv. |

  </div>

- <div class="block classTable frame" markdown="1">

  #### Activities

  | Pace | Hide Tracks | Reconnoiter | Stealth |
  |:-----|:------------:|:--------:|:------------:|
  | Careful | Adv. | Adv. | Normal |
  | Normal | Normal | Normal | Disadv. |
  | Reckless | Disadv. | Disadv. | None |

  </div>

- #### Exhaustion, -1 HA per 2 levels
  The most exhausted character in the party determines the penalty: every 2 levels of exhaustion they have reduces the group's HA by 1. A character with exhaustion level 5 or higher cannot Travel at all. The party can still Travel, but that character cannot participate in Travel activities (they must be carried, left behind, or otherwise accommodated). Some penalties can reduce the party’s available HAs below 1, but the party always has at least 1 HA unless special rules (such as being unable to travel due to exhaustion) say otherwise.

  If the party is traveling with mounts or vehicles, this penalty might be reduced or eliminated, depending on whether the mounts or vehicles are able to carry the exhausted party members and provide rest for them.

- #### Forced March, +1 HA

  This grants one extra hexploration activity for the day, but that HA is the only HA activity possible that day (no additional Travel, Reconnoiter or other HA‑based activities). The party still camps normally and may perform Pre‑camp, Large Rest and Small Rest activities as usual.

  Each character must make a Constitution saving throw at the end of the day. The DC is 10 + 5 for each day past the first in a row (i.e. DC 15 the second day, DC 20 the third day, etc.). On a failed saving throw, a character suffers one level of exhaustion and cannot benefit from a forced march the following day.

  A character with the **Endurance** skill, does not need to make a saving throw for the first day of a forced march, but must make a saving throw on subsequent days (then the DC would be 10 for the second day, 15 for the third day, etc.).

  If the party is traveling with mounts or vehicles, the check may be made with advantage, depending on whether the mounts or vehicles are able to carry the party's gear and provide rest for the party members.

- #### Severe Weather, -1 HA
  Heavy rain, snow, a blizzard or strong winds count as difficult conditions. Post-blizzard terrain also counts as difficult terrain until the snow clears, which may additionally increase the Travel activities required per hex.

- #### Special Skills, +1 HA
  Only one of the following skills can grant an extra hexploration activity per day, and only if the party has a character with that skill.

  A character with **Pathfinding** can deduce the most efficient route through untracked wilderness, effectively treating the terrain as if it had a road. This grants one extra hexploration activity for the day when traveling through wilderness terrain.

  If the party has a character with the **Mountain Routefinding** skill, they gain one extra hexploration activity for the day when traveling through mountainous terrain.

  There might be other skills that grant an extra hexploration activity, depending on the campaign and the GM's discretion.

</div>

### Hexploration Activities

Group activities require the whole party; each counts as one of the day's hexploration activities for everyone.

<div class="columnsthree">

- #### Travel

- The party travels toward an adjacent hex. Assign **Guide**, **Forager**, and **Lookout** roles as described in the [Travel rules](../RulesExtra/travel.html#internal-RolesDuringTravel).

- In open terrain, such as plains or grasslands, 1 Travel activity is enough to move from one hex to an adjacent hex. Traversing difficult terrain, such as a typical forest or desert, requires 2 Travel activities. Traversing greater difficult terrain, such as a steep mountain or typical swamp, requires 3 Travel activities.

- A road improves the terrain by one step. For example, a road over a mountain pass counts as difficult terrain instead of greater difficult terrain.

- Travel assumes that the party is moving overland on foot. If the party is flying or traveling on water, most hexes count as open terrain, although the GM may assign a higher terrain category when appropriate.

- Flying through a storm or strong winds counts as difficult or greater difficult terrain. Traveling downstream on a river counts as open terrain, while traveling upstream counts as difficult or greater difficult terrain.

- If there are HAs remaining after moving to the next hex, the party can continue traveling into another adjacent hex or perform other activities. If more HAs are required to move into the next hex than the party has available, the party can spend the remaining HAs to "travel closer" to the next hex, so that if they continue traveling the next day **to the same hex**, they will have already covered part of the distance.

- <div class="block classTable fillClassTable frame" markdown="1">

  | Terrain | HAs Required |
  |---|:---:|
  | Open terrain; plains, grasslands, downstream | 1 |
  | Difficult terrain; typical forest, desert, upstream | 2 |
  | Greater difficult terrain; steep mountain, typical swamp | 3 |
  | Road | -1 |

- #### Reconnoiter

  You spend time surveying and exploring a specific area, getting the lay of the land and looking for unusual features and specific sites. Reconnoitering a single hex takes a number of hexploration activities equal to the Travel cost for that hex: 1 HA for open terrain, 2 HAs for difficult terrain and 3 HAs for greater difficult terrain. Traveling on roads doesn’t lessen the time required to Reconnoiter.
  Once the hex has been Reconnoitered, you can Map the Area to reduce your chance of getting lost in that hex. You automatically find any special feature that doesn’t require a check to find, and you attempt the appropriate checks to find hidden special features.

  For instance, if you were looking for an obvious rock formation among some hills, you would spend 2 hexploration activities to Reconnoiter the hex, and you’d find the rock formation. But if you were looking for a hidden tengu monastery somewhere in some deep forests, after spending 2 activities to Reconnoiter the forest hex, you would have to succeed at a Perception check as part of your Reconnoiter activity to find the monastery.

  Skills that can be helpful when ***Reconnoitering*** a hex include **Investigation**, **Nature**, **Perception**, **Terrain Knowledge**, **Tracking**, **Scouting**, **Survival** and more. Depending on what there is to be found, the DM may call for other skills as well.

</div>

## Campsites

Camping means locating a safe site, performing useful activities, eating, resting, and completing daily preparations. Each character needs **one pound of food per day** and access to water - see [Travel - Planning a Journey](../RulesExtra/travel.html#internal-PlanningaJourney) for supply rules. Hunting, cooking, and some campsite activities are unavailable in dungeons or enclosed locations. Characters can choose different activities from the lists below. A character can only perform one activity from each category per day.

### Camp & Rest Activities

<div class="columnsthree">

- #### Pre-camp Activities

  Each character can perform **one Pre-camp Activity** when the party first arrives at camp for the day. These are things characters do immediately on arrival, before settling into rest.

- #### Large Rest Activities

  Each character can perform **one Large Rest Activity** during their rest period. These represent more involved tasks that take a significant portion of the evening or downtime.

- #### Small Rest Activities

  Each character can perform **one Small Rest Activity** during their rest period. These represent quicker, less demanding tasks that can be done alongside other downtime.

</div>

### Activities

The activities listed below are examples of what characters can do while at camp. Each activity falls into one or more of the categories defined above (**P**re-camp, **L**arge Rest, **S**mall Rest) and characters can only perform one activity from each category per day.

The only one that is required is the **Prepare Campsite** activity. The rest are optional and can be chosen based on the party's needs and the characters' abilities.

Some small rest activities have fractions or other limitations, indicating that they are not as efficient or effective as full rest activities.

#### Activity Descriptions

<div class="columnsthree">

- ##### Aid

  You pitch in to help another character with any Pre‑camp, Large Rest or Small Rest activity - holding the torch while they work, steadying the cooking pot, hauling brush for camouflage, assisting with site reconnaissance, carrying supplies, helping set up key defenses or other tasks. The first helper grants **advantage** to the lead character's check; each additional helper grants a further **+1 bonus**.

- ##### Camp Camouflage

  You work to keep the camp hidden while it's occupied; banking the fire to a low glow, screening light with cloaks or brush, positioning bedrolls in natural hollows and hushing the party. Make a **Wisdom (Survival)** check - having **Scouting** gives ***Expertise*** - or **Dexterity (Stealth)** at a higher DC. The better the concealment, the less likely something hunting in the dark will notice the warmth, the smell of food or the murmur of voices.

- ##### Craft Item

  You focus on crafting, following the normal downtime crafting rules. This represents an evening of focused work while at camp.

- ##### Cooking

  You prepare a special meal for the party beyond basic rations. Attempt a **Wisdom (Survival)** or **Intelligence (Cooking)** check; on a success, the meal provides a small benefit.

  <div class="block classTable fillClassTable frame" markdown="1">

  ##### Cooking Results
  | Check Result | Benefit |
  |:---:|-----------|
  | ≤ 5 | Some food got spoiled, 1d4 extra rations lost |
  | 6-15 | No effect |
  | 16-20 | You make something out of little; the party saves 1d4−1 rations (minimum 0). |
  | 21-25 | As 16-20, and each character who eats gains 2 temporary hit points until the next long rest starts. |
  | ≥ 26 | As 16-20, and each character who eats gains 5 temporary hit points and advantage on one saving throw against disease or poison before the next long rest starts. |

  </div>

- ##### Decipher Writing

  You attempt to read a magical or coded text. This is done following normal rules for deciphering.

- ##### Identify Alchemy

  You identify an alchemical item, determining its properties and potential uses.

- ##### Identify Magic

  You identify a magical item or effect, determining its properties and potential uses.

- ##### Influence NPC

  You attempt to improve an NPC companion's attitude. Make an appropriate social check (**Charisma (Persuasion)**, **Charisma (Deception)**, etc.) against a DC set by the DM. Success improves the NPC's attitude toward you. A failure may worsen it.

- ##### Learn Language

  You study and practice a new language during your downtime at camp. See [Languages](../RulesCharacter/languages.html) for details on available languages and the rules for learning them.

- ##### Fortify Camp

  Sharpened stakes along the perimeter, trip-lines between the trees, gear stowed where it can be grabbed in the dark - a well-fortified camp turns a patch of wilderness into something that at least resembles a defensible position. Make an appropriate **Intelligence (Craft)** check; the DC reflects the terrain and available materials. On a success, anyone keeping watch or defending the camp gains a +2 bonus to initiative rolls and **Perception** checks to detect approaching threats.

- ##### Map the Area

  You sit down with parchment and charcoal and commit what the party has learned to a proper map - trails, landmarks, hazards, the way the ridgeline bends. A good map means you'll never waste time second-guessing the route again. Make an **Intelligence (Investigation)** or **Wisdom (Survival)** check; on a success, the DC of any navigation check in that hex is permanently reduced by 2.

  The hex must have been successfully Reconnoitered at some point (not necessarily today). Once Reconnoitered, any character can later attempt to Map the Area as a Pre‑camp or Large Rest activity without needing to Reconnoiter again.

- ##### Organize Supplies

  You carefully organize the party's supplies, checking for spoilage and managing rations. Make an **Intelligence (Survival)** or **Wisdom (Survival)** check. On a success, reduce the risk of losing rations to spoilage or vermin for the next 24 hours (if a random event causes food loss, only lose half the rations you would normally lose).

- ##### Plan Next Day's Route

  You study your maps and notes and make small trips in different directions to be able to plan the most efficient route for tomorrow's travel. Make an **Intelligence (Nature)** or **Wisdom (Survival)** check using maps and notes. On a success, the party gains advantage on the Guide’s check for Travel through this hex on the next day or on checks made as part of Reconnoiter in this hex.

- ##### Prepare Campsite

  Attempt a **Wisdom (Survival)** check against the Zone DC (the DC set by the current hex/zone) or **Logistics** with Expertise if the character has it, reflecting their skill at organizing camp defenses and site selection. Once a campsite is prepared, you can use the Prepare Campsite activity in this same hex again in the future (but no more than once per 24 hours) to automatically achieve the same degree of success with only an hour of work.

  ***Success by more than 5*** The site is exceptionally well chosen. The first hour of camping activities has a reduced risk of random encounters.

  ***Success*** A serviceable campsite, suitable for camping activities.

  ***Failure*** A subpar site. Camping activities that require checks take a -2 penalty.

  ***Failure by more than 5*** The site is unusable for activities - only rest and daily preparations are possible.

  **Multiple attempts:** Multiple characters can each try to Prepare a Campsite; the group uses the best result. A character who critically fails may face any triggered encounter alone unless nearby allies choose to stay.

- ##### Repair Item

  You repair a damaged item or restore its durability. Make an appropriate **Intelligence (Craft)** check to repair a damaged item or restore durability.

- ##### Rest

  You focus purely on recovery during your rest. This allows extra focus on healing and meditation. If done as a Large Rest, you gain advantage on all healing and recovery rolls made during this rest. If done as a Small Rest, you gain advantage on one healing or recovery roll of your choice during this rest.

- ##### Scout Perimeter

  You scout the immediate area around the campsite, watching for signs of danger and potential threats. Make a **Wisdom (Survival)** or **Wisdom (Perception)** check. On a success, the party gains advantage on the first Perception check to detect ambushes or surprise attacks that night in this hex. On failure, no benefit; on a critical failure, the party has disadvantage on that first check.

- ##### Stand Guard

  While the others are busy cooking, camouflaging or tending wounds, someone needs to keep their eyes up and their hand near their weapon. You forgo other activities to stay alert, watching the treeline and listening for anything that shouldn't be there. Your **Perception** check is used if a threat approaches during the evening's activities - success means the party has time to react; failure means they're caught mid-task.

- ##### Trapping

  You set snares and traps around the campsite to catch game overnight. Make a **Wisdom (Survival)** check; if you have a relevant foraging, hunting, or trapping skill, you have **Expertise**. The DC is based on terrain. On a success, the traps yield enough food for **one character** the following day. For every 5 points by which the check exceeds the DC, the traps yield food for **one additional character** the next day. On a critical success, double the amount of food gained.

  Food is collected during the next day's Prepare Campsite or Breaking Camp activity; if the party leaves the hex before collecting, the traps and food are lost unless the DM rules otherwise. Multiple characters can take Trapping; each makes their own check and results stack.

- ##### Treat Wounds

  You provide medical care to an injured character. Make a **Wisdom (Medicine)** check to treat wounds, following normal healing rules for this activity.

</div>

### Resting

<div class="columnsthree">

- Once the party has eaten, it is time for a **Long Rest** with rotating watch assignments. A long rest is at least 8 hours in a safe place.

- <div class="block classTable fillClassTable frame" markdown="1">

  | Group Size | Total Time | Each Watch |
  |---|---|---|
  | 2 | 16 hours | 8 hours |
  | 3 | 12 hours | 4 hours |
  | 4 | 10 h 40 min | 2 h 40 min |
  | 5 | 10 hours | 2 hours |
  | 6 | 9 h 36 min | 1 h 36 min |
  | 7 | 9 h 20 min | 1 h 20 min |
  | 8 | 9 h 9 min | 1 h 9 min |
  | 9+ | 9 hours | 1 hour |

  </div>

- At the end of the long rest, each character gains the standard [Long Rest benefits](../RulesExtra/adventuring.html#internal-RestingLong): restored spell slots, Hit Dice recovery, and one chosen benefit (recover hit points, remove exhaustion, etc.). Sleeping in armor may reduce these benefits.

</div>

## Weather

The DM determines each day's weather during [Breaking Camp](../RulesExtra/travel.html#internal-BreakingCamp). Weather affects travel, visibility, foraging, and fatigue.

<div class="columnsthree">

- #### Precipitation

  Light rain or snow may occur during any season. Heavier precipitation and severe weather - downpours, blizzards, hailstorms, sandstorms, etc. - are determined by the DM and may also reduce the number of hexploration activities available for the day (see Activities per Day).

  When the party travels overland in precipitation or severe weather, for each hexploration activity (HA) they spend on Travel, each character must make a Constitution saving throw at the end of that activity or gain one level of exhaustion. The DC and associated Perception penalty depend on the severity of the weather:

- <div class="block classTable fillClassTable frame" markdown="1">

  | Weather | DC | Perception |
  |---|:-:|:-:|
  | Light Rain or Snow | 5 | -1 |
  | Heavy Rain or Snow | 10 | -2 |
  | Hailstorm | 15 | -4 |
  | Blizzard | 20 | -6 |
  | Sandstorm / Tornado / Hurricane | 25 | -8 |

  </div>

- #### Temperature

  Temperatures are typically mild in spring and autumn. During winter, cold snaps can occur.

  When they do: For each hexploration activity (HA) the party spends on overland travel, each character must make a Constitution saving throw at the end of that activity or gain one level of exhaustion. The DC is set by the DM based on severity. Characters with the **Arctic Survival** skill make this saving throw with advantage.

  Light precipitation falls as snow rather than rain. Characters without cold-weather gear may be subject to additional exposure rules at the DM’s discretion (for example, additional saves per hour of exposure when not actively traveling).

- #### Predict Weather

  Any character with **Wisdom (Survival)** can spend 10 minutes observing the sky, wind and wildlife at Breaking Camp to attempt to predict the day's weather. The DM sets the DC based on conditions - typically 15 in clear conditions, higher in poor visibility or unfamiliar terrain. Characters with a relevant terrain-specific Survival skill (such as **Forest Survival** or **Arctic Survival**) have ***Expertise***.

  ***Critical Success*** You accurately predict precipitation, temperature, wind, and whether a significant weather event will occur - what it is and roughly when. Advantage on Survival checks to prepare for it.

  ***Success*** You accurately predict precipitation, temperature, and wind, and know whether a weather event will occur, but not what or when. +1 to Survival checks to prepare for it.

  ***Failure*** No useful prediction.

  ***Critical Failure*** You reach a wrong conclusion. The DM gives you false information as though you had a critical success.

</div>
